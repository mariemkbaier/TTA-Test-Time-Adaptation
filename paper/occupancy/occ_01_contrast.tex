\subsection{One dataset, two preprocessings, opposite conclusions}
\label{sec:occ-contrast}

The occupancy data reaches us in two forms: an anonymised extract whose nine feature
columns are named $0$ to $8$, and the raw sensor files with named columns and timestamps.
They describe the same two offices and the same instrumentation. They do not agree about
which direction of transfer is broken.

On the anonymised extract, transfer one way is strong and the other way is worse than
chance. On the raw files, with the protocol held fixed so that the data version is the
only thing that differs, the broken direction is the other one, and it is not reliably
broken but straddling chance: mean $-0.0711$ with six of ten seeds negative and a range
from $-0.42$ to $+0.21$. Restricted to the seven features of
Section~\ref{sec:occ-features}, the two directions become
indistinguishable, $+0.3388$ against $+0.3597$.

We could not reconcile the two. No data-preparation script exists in either published
repository; the loader in the more recent one keeps every non-label column, which yields
twelve and ten features rather than nine, and applies no binarisation; and the source
publications describe a three-level occupancy protocol rather than a binary one. Row
counts differ in both directions ($1210$ against $1345$, $1041$ against $972$) and no
binarisation threshold reproduces both label balances.

The disagreement is the point, and it is why we report it here rather than as a
limitation. Section~\ref{sec:res-leak} showed that a cross-building penalty can be
manufactured by a validation split; this shows the same quantity inverted by a
preprocessing step, on a different task, a different dataset and a different sensing
modality. In both cases the artefact is invisible in the reported numbers and recoverable
only by re-deriving them from a more primitive form of the data. A cross-domain penalty is
not a measurement of two environments until the pipeline between the sensors and the
table has been audited, and the two instances together suggest that is not a rare
requirement.

Everything that follows uses the raw extracts, which carry the timestamps and column names
that make the audit possible. The anonymised results are retained in
Table~\ref{tab:occmethods} as the prior extract, labelled as such, so the contrast above
can be read against them.

\subsection{A feature that predicts occupancy here and the opposite there}
\label{sec:occ-features}

Two of the nine shared columns correlate with occupancy in one office and anti-correlate
in the other, and one of them is the strongest single predictor the source office has.
While the columns were named $0$ to $8$ this was not a question anyone could ask.

\texttt{rms} is an acoustic measure. In the source office it is the best single predictor
of occupancy in the table at $+0.626$; in the target office it points the other way at
$-0.202$, with non-overlapping intervals. \texttt{derivative\_co2}, the rate of change of
carbon dioxide, does the same more weakly.

It is worth being precise about what kind of defect this is, because the obvious
description is wrong. These are not noisy or uninformative features to be pruned.
\texttt{rms} carries real signal where it was recorded: removing it and
\texttt{derivative\_co2} drops the source office's own-days MCC from $0.7643$ to $0.3624$
(Table~\ref{tab:occpenalty}), so almost half of what the source office knows about its own
occupancy is carried by a feature that means the opposite thing next door. The feature is
genuinely predictive at home and genuinely misleading away. That combination is what makes
it invisible to any evaluation conducted in one building, and it is the reason the defect
survives into a cross-building result rather than being caught upstream.

The two offices also do not instrument the same things. The source office has
\texttt{window}, \texttt{power5} and \texttt{power6}; the target has \texttt{power4}. Of
the three power channels nominally shared, \texttt{power3} is zero in $96\%$ of source
rows and $5\%$ of target rows, median $0$ against $11$: the column name is shared and the
appliance behind it is not. This is the per-sensor result of
Section~\ref{sec:res-feat} with placement rather than index as the thing that fails to
transfer.

\paragraph{The penalty, measured against the right ceiling.} The raw files carry
\texttt{time}, which gives a recording-day index --- ten days in the source office, eight
in the target --- so the measurement of Section~\ref{sec:res-gap} becomes available for
the first time on this task: hold the evaluation days fixed, vary only which office
supplied the training labels, match training-set size.

Dropping the two reversing columns cuts the penalty into the source office from $47.2$
points to $7.6$. Most of what presented as a cross-office domain gap in that direction was
two features meaning opposite things in the two rooms. Into the target office a penalty of
$44.5$ points survives the same surgery, so unlike the activity task this pair of
environments does contain a real shift --- in one direction, and smaller than the
uncorrected figure suggests.
